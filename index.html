1. Fundamentals & Classifications

What is Type 2 Diabetes?

Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition characterized by high blood glucose levels resulting from insulin resistance (cells failing to respond properly to insulin) combined with a progressive decline in insulin secretion.

What is the Difference Between Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes?

  • Type 1 Diabetes: An autoimmune condition where the immune system destroys insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. It requires lifelong insulin therapy and cannot be reversed through lifestyle changes.
  • Type 2 Diabetes: Linked heavily to metabolic health, lifestyle, and ectopic fat deposition. It accounts for over 90-95% of cases and can often be put into remission.

2. Type 2 Diabetes Reversal & Remission

What is Diabetes "Remission" (Reversal)?

According to major medical consensus, diabetes remission is achieved when an individual maintains an HbA1c level of less than 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) for at least 3 months without the use of any glucose-lowering medications.

Can Type 2 diabetes be completely cured?

Medical professionals prefer the term "remission." While blood sugar levels can normalize completely without medication, the underlying metabolic susceptibility remains. If significant weight is regained or older lifestyle patterns return, blood sugar levels may rise again.

Who is a good candidate for diabetes remission?

Individuals diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes relatively recently (within the last 5 to 10 years), those with remaining pancreatic beta-cell function, and those committed to structured changes in nutrition, physical activity, and weight management.

3. Diet, Exercise & Blood Glucose Tracking

What role does diet play in reversing Type 2 diabetes?

Nutrition is foundational. Low-carbohydrate diets, whole foods, and calorie management reduce circulating insulin levels, forcing the body to burn visceral fat stored around the liver and pancreas, which restores natural insulin sensitivity.

How does physical activity help lower blood sugar?

Exercise causes muscles to uptake glucose directly from the bloodstream without needing insulin. Over time, regular physical training increases overall insulin sensitivity, making metabolic management significantly easier.

Why is tracking blood glucose important during lifestyle changes?

Regular monitoring helps identify how specific foods, stress levels, sleep quality, and physical activities impact individual blood sugar responses, providing real-time feedback to optimize the reversal journey safely.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

Can Type 1 diabetes be reversed?

No. Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune disease requiring lifelong insulin replacement therapy.

Is medical supervision required when attempting diabetes remission?

Yes, absolutely. Rapid changes in diet and weight combined with glucose-lowering medications can lead to dangerous hypoglycemia (low blood sugar). Doctors must safely taper and adjust medications as blood sugar levels improve.
